Zeke understands subtraction. Give him 98 minus 83 and he knows what to do. He just did not want to do it anymore, and the moment we switched to regrouping, the whole topic stalled.

More worksheets were not going to fix that. The problem was not the answer. It was the step in the middle, the part where a ten has to break open before the ones can subtract. So we backed up one step instead of pushing forward.

## Break it apart first

The idea is simple, and it is the whole post in one sentence: split both numbers into tens and ones, subtract the parts, then put the answer back together.

Take $38 - 14$, the problem our practice tool opens with. Split 38 into $30 + 8$ and 14 into $10 + 4$. Subtract the tens, $30 - 10 = 20$. Subtract the ones, $8 - 4 = 4$. Put it back together, $20 + 4 = 24$.

Nothing new is being taught here. It is the same subtraction Zeke already knows, written out so the thinking is visible. And when regrouping arrives, the only new move is one he has already practiced in a gentler form: if the ones will not subtract, break a ten open first. So 32 becomes $20 + 12$ instead of $30 + 2$, and the ones finally have enough to work with.

## A free tool that plays like a puzzle

I built a free interactive tool for exactly this, because Zeke was done with methods and worksheets, and he needed something that felt like a game he was racing.

[Try the Break-Apart Subtraction tool](https://redwolflearning.github.io/site/break-apart-subtraction/)

One card appears at a time, laid out just like the paper card he already uses. He fills in the splits and the answer, then checks the whole card at once. Anything that is off gets highlighted so he can fix it himself, and a clean card grows his streak.

There are three modes, so it grows with him: Without Regrouping, With Regrouping, and Mixed. Without Regrouping is the default, and it will never sneak in a regrouping problem. With Regrouping is the one waiting for his regrouping lesson.

## Take the game home

The tool is the practice. The printable is the take-home.

[Download the free Break-Apart Subtraction Race game](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/Break-Apart-Subtraction-Race-Game-FREEBIE-2-Digit-Subtraction-Grades-1-2-18378299)

It is a 10-page printable with two game boards, 20 cards, a spinner page with two spinners to choose from, and an answer key. Sixteen cards need no regrouping, and four give a small taste of it. The board is a 28-space race from START to FINISH, with Break-a-Ten Bonus stars on spaces 7, 15, and 23 on the Star Board. The Starter Board has no stars, so it plays with just the 16 no-regrouping cards. Solve a card, spin the spinner, and move. A wrong card just goes to the bottom of the deck and comes back around. Nothing ever moves backward.

You can play it with our [online spinner](https://redwolflearning.github.io/site/spinner/) too. It uses the same 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3 mix as the printable spinner, so a game takes about ten or more correct cards to finish, which is exactly the practice volume we want.

One setup tip from our table: laminate the boards, or print them on cardstock, and they will hold up race after race. The cards are worth laminating too. Our first run, Zeke wrote right on the paper cards, and they were done after one game. Laminated, the same cards take a dry-erase marker, wipe clean, and play again.

## How we are using it this week

Short bursts, not long sessions. A few cards on the tool, a quick race on the board, then we stop while it still feels like a game. That is the plan ahead of his regrouping lesson, and it is already working better than another page of problems did.

If your child is stuck in the same place, where the subtraction makes sense and the regrouping does not, try backing up one step with them. Build the bridge first, and let the regrouping walk across it.
