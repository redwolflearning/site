The laminator was one of the first tools I bought when we started homeschooling. Not the curriculum, not the fancy manipulatives. A laminator. I went with the [9-inch Scotch](https://amzn.to/4itBHc5), and it has earned its keep a hundred times over.

Here's why: every week I print things Zeke needs more than once. Reference sheets, practice pages, charts. Without lamination, I'd be reprinting the same pages constantly as they got crumpled, spilled on, or loved to death. With it, I print once and the page survives the whole unit.

## What I actually laminate

Not everything. Pouches cost money, so I'm strategic. What earns lamination is anything that gets handled daily or needs to survive more than one use:

**Reference sheets that live on the table.** Zeke's blank hundreds chart and his hundreds-tens-ones place value chart are both laminated now. They sit next to him during math, he writes on them with a dry-erase marker, wipes them clean, and they look brand new every day. Before lamination, I was reprinting these every couple of weeks.

**Worksheets worth repeating.** Some pages deserve a second or third run: number bonds, math facts, handwriting practice. Laminated plus a dry-erase marker, and one printed page becomes a week's worth of practice instead of a single use.

That's the real trick, and it's almost embarrassingly simple: lamination plus dry-erase markers turns any worksheet into a reusable one. No more printing five copies of the same fact sheet.

## What I learned about pouches

I'll keep this simple: I only use the 3 mil pouches, and they've held up fine for everything, including the stuff Zeke handles every day. The machine has two temperature settings for the two thicknesses, so just match the setting to your pouch and feed the page in straight.

One more tip from experience: let it fully warm up before you feed anything. Impatience makes bubbles.

## The honest part: I'd buy the wide one today

Here's my confession. I bought the 9-inch, and it does everything I need. Letter-size sheets go through perfectly, and I've never once thought it was a bad purchase.

But every so often I print something bigger, an anchor chart or a full-page number line, and I have to trim it down or split it across two sheets. And then there's the real reason I wish I'd gone bigger: Zeke's art. He makes these big, wonderful creations, and I want to save my favorites. They don't fit through the 9-inch, and trimming a kid's artwork to fit your laminator feels wrong.

If I were buying today, I'd get the [Scotch Wide Thermal Laminator](https://amzn.to/4senNhf) instead. It takes 13-inch input, so 11x17 sheets go through in one pass, and it has a pouch release lever for the rare jam. Right now it's about the same price as the 9-inch, so there's honestly no reason not to go bigger.

So that's my genuine recommendation: the [9-inch](https://amzn.to/4itBHc5) if you catch it on sale, the [wide one](https://amzn.to/4senNhf) if you want to stop thinking about size limits. Either way, your printer is about to become a curriculum factory.
