Thanksgiving week is coloring page season. Every teacher and homeschool parent I know reaches for them, including me. They keep little hands busy during the busiest week of the fall, and there is nothing wrong with that.

But most coloring pages are just coloring. I wanted ones that would keep the math going too, without feeling like a worksheet in disguise. So I made a set where the coloring only works if the math does.

## How it works

Each section of the picture holds a number sentence with a missing part, like $3 + \underline{\phantom{3}} = 6$. Your child solves it, finds that answer in the crayon key at the bottom, and colors the section to match. If a color looks wrong, the picture itself tells them to go back and check their work. The pages grade themselves, which makes them perfect for morning work, math centers, early finishers, or the homeschool table while you are getting the pie in the oven.

Tiny details that could never fit a whole number sentence, like the turkey's beak and feet, carry just the key number. And a few parts have no number at all, so there is still room to color something any color they like.

## Two pages, two levels

**The pumpkin page** is the gentle start: 9 number sentences with wholes of 5, 6, and 7, and only 3 colors in the key. This is the one for kindergarten, or for any child who is still building confidence with missing parts.

**The turkey page** is the step up: more than 30 number sentences with wholes up to 10 and 5 colors in the key. This one suits 1st grade, or a kindergartner who is ready for a challenge. At home, that split means siblings at different levels can work side by side on the same activity, which is exactly how it goes at our table.

## Why the missing part matters

Finding the missing part is part-part-whole thinking, the foundation under addition and subtraction. A child who can look at 5 and know it is 2 and 3 is building the number sense that later makes regrouping and mental math click. It is the same skill my [number bonds post](https://redwolflearning.github.io/site/2026/05/29/number-sense-with-number-bonds.html) is all about, and the same one Zeke practices on our [interactive number bond tool](https://redwolflearning.github.io/site/number-bond-tool/). These coloring pages are the quiet, crayon version of that work.
