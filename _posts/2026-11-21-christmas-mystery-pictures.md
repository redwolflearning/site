In November I posted two Thanksgiving coloring pages that sneak in missing part practice, and teachers downloaded them more than a hundred times in the first few weeks. The message was clear: December needed a version too. So this time I built the whole set at once.

Six Christmas pictures are hiding in grids of numbers: a tree, a stocking, an ornament, a wreath, a bell, and a candle. The tree came first, hand-colored at my kitchen table, and then the whole family got pulled in.

The bell is my daughter's design, start to finish, her picture entirely. My son was all about testing them out, coloring his way through every page as I finished it. The set passed its first test run at our own table before it ever reached a store.

## How a mystery picture works

Each page is a grid full of numbers, plus a color code. In one format the code is number bonds with a part missing. In the other it is addition equations with an addend missing. Students solve for the missing number, then color every square with that number to match the code.

The picture only comes out right when the math is right, so the page checks itself. A wrong answer does not hide. It shows up as a green square where the stocking should be red, and students go looking for their mistake on their own.

## Two formats, same six pictures

[The number bonds edition](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/Christmas-Mystery-Pictures-Number-Bonds-Kindergarten-1st-Grade-Math-18416328) practices part-part-whole thinking: here is the whole, here is one part, what is the missing part?

[The missing part equations edition](https://www.teacherspayteachers.com/Product/-Christmas-Mystery-Pictures-Missing-Part-Equations-K-1st-Grade-Math-18453394) practices the same facts as missing addends, written the friendly way students meet first, known part + __ = whole.

Every number stays within 10 in both. Because the pictures are the same, the two editions sequence well: bonds first while the idea is new, equations when students are ready to see the same thinking written as a number sentence. They also differentiate quietly. Two students can color the same stocking at the same table, one from bonds and one from equations, at the level each one needs.
